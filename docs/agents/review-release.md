# Review a release candidate from one angle

**Input**: an angle sub-item of a release issue, the brief, the evidence pack, the candidate tag. **Output**: a PR from `qa/<candidate>/<angle>` to the release branch that carries your report file and nothing else: you fix nothing.

1. Cut your branch: `git switch -c qa/<candidate>/<angle> <candidate tag>` on the release branch's history.
2. Read the brief (embarked tickets and their acceptance criteria, business rules touched, evidence pack) and the tag annotation (release class). Then review the **whole project** from your angle, not the diff: the diff orients you to what changed; your scope is the current state. Run what you need to run: suites, the application, scripts. Record in `checked` what you actually examined.
3. For each finding, decide: **blocking** (must not ship) or **non-blocking** (real, later). Your angle's file grades its findings for a PR review: there, a `blocker` is blocking, a `should-fix` or a `nit` non-blocking. Each finding you document, you do not solve: `summary` what you observed, `anchor` where, `evidence` in what context (how you met it, what it looks like), as faithfully as you can, since whoever takes its ticket up has those words alone; never hunt for its fix (you may end `evidence` with one already obvious). A blocking finding becomes an urgent fix ticket, assigned to whoever fixes release problems (`release.fix_assignee`), and holds the GO until its fix, merged to the release branch, makes a later candidate. A non-blocking finding becomes a ticket logged for later.
4. Write the report at the path your sub-item names, `.spl-asdlc/qa/<candidate>/<angle>.json` by default (`release.record`; schema and rules in `docs/reference/06-qa-report.md`, which your sub-item embeds after this protocol). It is mandatory even with no finding: `checked` says what you looked at, `findings` is empty, `verdict` is `go`. In it, each finding is `"blocking": true` or `"blocking": false`, and the verdict is `hold` when one is blocking. Its `item` is your sub-item's key (such as `ABC-123`), never your name or anyone's: the report is committed to the repository, and the tracker knows who holds the sub-item.
5. Commit the report, push, open the PR to the release branch; it carries no fix. Body: your sub-item's id, the verdict, the count of blocking and non-blocking findings. The coordinator reviews your PR as it comes, as its reviewer; answer its threads; the bridge merges it once the coordinator approved it at its head.

Your sub-item follows your PR, like any work item (BR-ASDLC-020): **In Progress** once your PR is opened (a draft PR is out of the flow: the bridge ignores it until it is marked ready); **In Review** once its required checks are green, assigned to the coordinator, who is then served your PR's review; back **In Progress** when the coordinator requests changes at its head, assigned to your angle's configured assignee again (or to nobody, taken by role), until you push the corrected report; **Done** when the bridge merges the PR. A PR whose title or body does not name your sub-item is neither followed nor merged; the bridge says so once on the PR, with the key to name. The candidate's overall review (`docs/agents/consolidate.md`) starts only once every angle's PR is merged.

You never write in the tracker: the bridge creates the tickets from your report.
