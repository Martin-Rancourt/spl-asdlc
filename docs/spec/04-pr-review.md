# 04 — PR review

Fast, on the diff, with several angles and one coordinator. The volume is tens of PRs a day: nothing here may drown the PR or the tracker. The review is the project's choice: a project may configure no PR reviewer, and its PRs then merge on their CI (§11).

## Invariants

1. **CI first.** A PR is reviewed only when it is ready (not draft), its required checks are green, and its branch is up to date with its base when the base requires it. The content of the CI is the project's (build, tests, lint, project guards); the method only requires a single aggregated required check whose failure or skip blocks. **The ticket is In Review as soon as its PR is opened**, before CI: the tick that finds a PR opened (not a draft, no coordinator's request for changes at its head, no review round of its own yet) moves its ticket to In Review, from In Progress, or straight from Backlog or Ready when nobody marked it started: In Progress is the implementer's, agent or person, who starts the ticket, and the bridge never writes it as a backup. A **draft** is work in progress: it leaves the ticket where it is (In Progress once started; a draft on a ticket still Ready writes nothing), and a PR put back in draft before its round opened sends the ticket back In Progress, unless another PR of the ticket is in review (§10). A draft is never reviewed nor merged; a PR put in draft during its round leaves the ticket In Review and holds the merge.
2. **The ticket's contract gates the review.** The linked ticket must have a complete contract and at least one box ticked (`01-ticket-contract.md`). Otherwise the PR waits and its author is told once.
3. **The bridge opens the review.** When 1 and 2 hold, the bridge creates one **angle sub-item** per configured angle plus one **consolidation item**, all linked to the PR: the PR's **review round**. The ticket is already In Review (1); the round's opening writes In Review only for a ticket sent back In Progress (§5), whose new head earns a new round. Being sub-items of the ticket, they are never taken for the PR's ticket, even when an agent mangled their marker (`01-ticket-contract.md` §7). The consolidation item is created with the round, so the whole round is visible from the start, but **blocked by every angle sub-item of the round** through the tracker's own `blocks` relation, which dispatchers respect: it is served only once every angle sub-item is resolved (Done or Canceled), when the tracker stops counting the relation as blocking (BR-ASDLC-018). The bridge assigns each item at its creation: an angle sub-item to its angle's `assignee`, the consolidation item to the coordinator named by `review.coordinator_assignee` (an agent the tracker serves, like an angle's reviewer; `08-configuration.md`); an unset or unknown participant leaves the item unassigned, and an existing item is never reassigned, since a human or an agent may have taken it. The relations are written right after the items and before the round comment (and In Review, for a ticket sent back); a relation that cannot be written holds the round's opening and is retried at the next tick, and an open round missing a relation gets it at the next tick. A round is scoped to its PR: a ticket already In Review for another of its PRs keeps its status and the new PR gets its own round. Which angles apply is configuration: each angle of `[[review.angles]]` applies to every PR, or, when it carries `paths`, only to a PR that touches one of them (a security angle on the workflows and the authentication code, a design angle on the front end), and the size of the diff may add one more (possibly none at all, §11). A PR whose diff cannot be read gets every angle that carries paths, since whether it touches them is unknown, and is held until the diff is read. A PR that gets no angle has no round (§11).
    **Each item carries its protocol** (BR-ASDLC-015): the agent served it never depends on reading the method's repository, which is absent from the project's. Beside its contract (goal, non-goals, acceptance criteria, business rules), an item says what closes it in the bridge's terms and embeds, under a `## Protocol` section whose headings are demoted below the contract's, the method's protocol of its job: an angle sub-item `docs/agents/review-pr.md` and the angle's file `docs/agents/angles/<angle>.md` (an angle of the project's own, which the method has no file for, gets the generic protocol and a line saying no method notes exist for it); the consolidation item `docs/agents/consolidate.md`. The project's angle notes (`notes` of the angle) stay in the project's repository: the item links them. The texts are compiled into the bridge, so an item carries the protocol of the bridge version that created it, and keeps it: an item is found again by its marker, which holds no protocol text, or by its exact title among the ticket's children, so that an edited marker never makes the bridge create it twice, and is never rewritten. An angle sub-item closes when a submitted review on the PR names its key in its body (§4); the consolidation item closes with the round when the PR resolves (§6, §10).
4. **Findings live in the PR.** Each reviewer reads the diff from its angle, with the ticket's contract and the business rules touched as context, and posts its findings as review comments on the PR (one thread per finding, severity in the first line). A human who comments on the PR is one more angle. The tracker never receives a copy; the sub-item is closed by the bridge when the reviewer's review is submitted, a review whose body names the sub-item's key. The reviewer knows a PR may not close its ticket, and checks that it is deployable on its own (§10); a PR that is not self-standing, or that should have been one PR with another, is a `blocker` finding whose reason the reviewer also records in the ticket's comments.
5. **The coordinator fixes and approves.** Once every angle has submitted, the consolidation item is served: its blocking relations lift as the bridge closes each angle sub-item (§3, BR-ASDLC-018). The coordinator reads every thread, rejects false alarms with a reason in the thread, applies the retained fixes as commits on the PR branch, resolves the threads it has handled, and submits an approving review. If the change needed is beyond a fix (a confirmed finding that the PR is not deployable on its own, or should have been one PR, included), it requests changes and the ticket goes back to the implementer (In Progress), unless another PR of the ticket is in review (§10). The implementer pushes; the round of the new head, once its CI is green, brings the ticket back to In Review. On a PR with no angle (§11), a request for changes from a coordinator at the head sends the ticket back In Progress the same way, and the PR opened at a new head brings it back to In Review at once.
6. **CI again, then the bridge merges.** The merge requires, for a PR whose current plan gets an angle (§11): coordinator approval at the head SHA, no angle sub-item of the PR's round still open (an approval given while one is open does not count, even when a dispatcher ignored the relation; the PR is told once that it waits for the angle reviews, BR-ASDLC-018), required checks green at that SHA, no unresolved thread, branch up to date if required, no standing request for changes from a person, one merge per repository per tick. A request for changes stands, whatever the head it was given on, until it is dismissed or its author reviews again (GitHub keeps it blocking the merge). Besides the coordinators' own (which hold the PR, §5): a **person's** holds the merge, and the bridge never dismisses it; the tick says `waiting: pr#<n> changes requested by <login> (a person; the bridge never dismisses it)`. An **App's** (an author `<slug>[bot]` once normalised, as the coordinators are matched) is superseded by the coordinator's approval at the head: when every other condition holds, the bridge dismisses each such review right before the merge, with a message naming the coordinator's approval and the head SHA, then merges. Without that approval nothing is dismissed. Each dismissal is in the action log and the dry run; a dismissed review is no longer standing, so a replay dismisses nothing again, and a dismissal that fails holds the merge until the next tick. The merge is a squash by default (configurable), pinned to the head SHA; the branch is deleted; the PR's round closes; the ticket goes where §10 says: **To Deploy** when every box is ticked and no other PR is open (**Done** for a ticket with nothing to deploy).
7. **The merge is idempotent and observable.** The bridge records every merge decision in its action log; a PR merged outside the bridge is swept and given the same transitions, once.
8. **Exclusions.** PRs of the release machinery (merge-backs and their conflict resolutions, angle PRs of a release QA) are never reviewed by angles and never merged by this path; `05-release-cycle.md` governs them.
9. **PRs of Apps that need no ticket.** A PR is linked to exactly one ticket, which a human or an agent writes; a PR authored by an App the project lists (`review.ticketless_authors`, for example Dependabot) gets its ticket from the bridge instead. When such a PR is linked to no ticket, the bridge opens one: labelled `ticketless-pr`, with a complete contract whose one box the PR meets, linked to the PR. From the next tick the PR takes the path of every PR (review round, merge, To Deploy, release, closure). Only an App qualifies, never a human whose login matches. When such a PR is closed without merge (an App superseding its own PR), the bridge cancels the ticket it opened and that ticket's open sub-items; a PR reopened later stays on that ticket until a person sets it back to Ready. Any other PR without a ticket waits, and its author is told once.
10. **A ticket and its PRs.** One PR per ticket is the default, with as many commits as needed; several only for a real reason, recorded by the implementer in the ticket's comments. **Every PR branches from its base** (`main`, or the release branch for a hotfix) and is never stacked on another PR's branch; a ticket's later PR also starts from the base, once the earlier one is merged. With a squash merge, a stacked PR carries the other PR's diff into the base under its own merge, and the bridge never sees that other PR, nor its ticket, merged. **Every PR is independently deployable**: a merged PR ships with the next release whatever its ticket's state, even when it does not close the ticket; the release embarks the ticket and the closure judges it only once it is To Deploy (`05-release-cycle.md` §11). **A review round is scoped to its PR**: its angle sub-items and its consolidation item close when the PR resolves (Done at a merge, Canceled at a close without merge), while the ticket stays open for a later PR. Whenever a PR of a ticket resolves, and whenever a ticket past the merge (To Deploy, Done) has an open PR, the bridge sets the ticket's state by these rules, in order:
    1. **The review rule** has priority: another PR of the ticket in review (opened, not a draft, not sent back; its round open or not yet opened) keeps or puts the ticket **In Review**, and that PR's review → deploy cycle runs in full; a PR of the ticket the coordinator sent back (changes requested at its head) puts it **In Progress**.
    2. **The completeness rule**: with no PR in review, a ticket whose boxes are not all ticked (every box of its description under any heading, the `## Post deploy` section set apart; a ticket that declares no box is not complete) goes back **In Progress**. An implementer resumes; its next PR brings the ticket to In Review again, until every box is ticked.
    3. **A draft PR on a complete ticket** (REQ-MERGE-028 as corrected, below the two rules): the ticket is **In Progress**, a draft being work in progress.
    4. Otherwise, a PR of the ticket merged: **To Deploy**, waiting for the next release (every project has the cycle, `05-release-cycle.md`); none merged (a PR closed without merge): **In Progress**.

    **Nothing to deploy.** A ticket that carries the no-deploy label (`tracker.no_deploy_label`, default `no-deploy`; a person or refinement adds it, the bridge only reads it) has nothing to deploy, documentation say: its merge delivers it. Where rule 4 says To Deploy it goes **Done**, by the Done gate the closure uses (its boxes and its duplicates'), or **Post Deploy** with its validation sub-task while only its own `## Post deploy` boxes are open, or In Progress while a box of a duplicate is open; and no release embarks it (`05-release-cycle.md` §2): no release label, no judgement at the closure. Without the label, To Deploy as above.

    A ticket in Post Deploy is not reopened. When the ticket of an open PR could not be read, it may be this ticket's: nothing but "another PR is in review" can be said, so the bridge writes nothing and decides at the next tick.
11. **Review is optional per project.** A project may run the method with no PR review: it writes `angles = []` under `[review]`, an explicit choice (a file that says nothing about its angles is invalid, `08-configuration.md`). A PR whose plan gets **no angle** — no angle on every PR, none whose paths it touches, none added by its size — has **no review round**: the bridge creates no angle sub-item and no consolidation item, and needs no coordinator approval; its ticket went In Review when the PR was opened, as any (§1). It merges as soon as it is mergeable on its CI: required checks green (1), the ticket's contract complete with a box ticked (2), the business-rule guard, nothing that holds it (an unread diff or a skipped required check), no unresolved thread, the branch up to date when required, one merge per repository per tick; a standing request for changes from a configured coordinator, or from a person, still holds it; with no approval, nothing is dismissed (§6). When `human_approval` asks for a human (`always`), the human's approval is then the only one required: the PR waits and a human is asked once, as in §6. The merge, the deleted branch and the ticket's state follow §6 and §10 as for any PR (To Deploy when every box is ticked); the action log, and so `tick` and its dry run, say the merge was made with no PR review. Angles that triggers add still apply: in a project with no angle on every PR, a PR that touches the `paths` of an angle, or that is over a configured size threshold (off by default), gets its round and needs the coordinator's approval, so `review.coordinators` is required whenever some PR can get an angle. `doctor` reports `PR review: none` when no PR can. **The plan is the current configuration's**: it is computed at every tick from the configuration the bridge reads then, and the merge conditions follow it, never the configuration a round was opened under. A PR whose plan gets no angle merges on its CI as above **whatever round is open**: a round opened under an earlier configuration (before `angles = []`, say) no longer applies, and the bridge cancels its open items — angle sub-items and consolidation item — before the merge, each with a comment saying the configuration no longer asks for it; the coordinator's approval is then not required. A PR whose plan gets fewer angles than its open round has keeps the rest of its round: the angle sub-items of the angles no longer asked are canceled the same way, the others and the consolidation item stand, and the coordinator's approval is still required (§6). An angle is taken as no longer asked only on a diff read in full: an unread diff matched no angle's paths, so it cancels nothing. Each item is commented once and then Canceled; a cancel that fails holds the merge until the next tick, which never comments the item again. Release QA is configured on its own (`05-release-cycle.md` §3): no PR review says nothing about a release's angles.

## What the reviewer receives

- The diff, the PR description, the linked ticket's goal and acceptance criteria, and the ticket's comments, where its implementer records why it has more than one PR (§10).
- The business rules touched by the diff (the rule files of `.spl-asdlc/business-rules/` the diff changes, or whose test files or cited paths it intersects) and the registry id format.
- Its angle protocol (`docs/agents/review-pr.md` and `docs/agents/angles/<angle>.md`), embedded in its sub-item (§3), and a link to the project's angle notes if any.
- The severity scale and the verdict vocabulary (`03-vocabulary.md`).

## What the coordinator receives

Everything the reviewers received, plus every review thread, the CI status, and the consolidation protocol (`docs/agents/consolidate.md`, embedded in the consolidation item, §3): every finding is confirmed or rejected with a reason, exactly once; a rejected finding is answered in its thread; a confirmed one is fixed on the branch or sent back to the implementer.

## Configuration (per project)

- The angles (`angles = []` for none: no PR review, §11), each on every PR or on its `paths`, each with an optional assignee hint.
- The coordinators whose approval merges a reviewed PR (`review.coordinators`), required only when some PR can get an angle (§11), and the tracker participant who receives the consolidation item (`review.coordinator_assignee`), optional.
- Merge method (`squash` | `merge`), whether the branch must be up to date, the diff size above which an extra angle is added (none by default).
- Whether a human must approve in addition to the coordinator, or alone for a PR with no angle (`review.human_approval`: `always` | `never`).
- The Apps whose PRs need no ticket (§9).
