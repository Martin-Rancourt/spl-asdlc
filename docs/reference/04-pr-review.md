# 04 — PR review

Fast, on the diff, with several angles and one coordinator. The volume is tens of PRs a day: nothing here may drown the PR or the tracker. The review is the project's choice: a project may configure no PR reviewer, and its PRs then merge on their CI (§11).

## Ready contract gate

One ticket ships in one PR. If a technical constraint requires splitting the
work, create sibling tickets linked by `blocked by`, each with its own PR.
On every pass that finds a ready PR, before waiting for CI, the bridge requires
every ticket box outside `## Post deploy` to be checked. A line linked to an
unfinished child also counts as unfinished, even if manually checked. Fenced
examples are ignored; post-deploy subheadings belong to the exemption.
The bridge lists the missing lines on the ticket and PR, returns the ticket to
In Progress and the PR to draft. Complete the work, check its boxes and mark
the PR ready again: the gate reads the ticket again, even on the same head.

## Invariants

1. **CI first.** A PR is reviewed only when it is ready (not a draft), its required checks are green, and its branch is up to date with its base when the base requires it. The content of the CI is the project's (build, tests, lint, project guards), but for the method's own checks, which every project runs: the PR title, and the `guards` job, which also refuses a PR whose `.spl-asdlc/process.md` is not the process page of the version its callers pin (BR-ASDLC-028). The method requires two checks, the aggregated `required-check`, whose failure or skip blocks and which always aggregates the guards (BR-ASDLC-024), and the title check: the bridge counts a required check green only by a success, skipped, neutral, missing or still running it waits (GitHub reports a skipped job as passing a required check). **The ticket is In Review as soon as its PR is opened**, before CI: the tick that finds a PR opened (no coordinator's request for changes at its head, no review round of its own yet) moves its ticket to In Review, from In Progress, or straight from Backlog or Ready when nobody marked it started: In Progress is the implementer's, agent or person, who starts the ticket, and the bridge never writes it as a backup. **A draft PR is out of the flow**: the bridge ignores it entirely. It writes no status from it (the ticket stays where it is, whatever its state, a ticket past the merge included), opens no review round for it, never merges it, does not read its CI, and counts it for nothing in the rules on a ticket's several PRs (§10); the tick's report lists it as `waiting: pr#<n> it is a draft, out of the flow until it is marked ready`. A PR put in draft during its round leaves the round as it is, and the round goes on once the PR is marked ready again. A person takes a PR out of the flow by putting it in draft, and back in by marking it ready (`09-requalification-and-human-gestures.md`). No job of the PR workflow runs on a draft, `required-check` included (each has `if: github.event.pull_request.draft == false`), so a draft costs no runner minute; the `required-check` action also fails should it ever run on one. GitHub reports those jobs skipped on the draft's head, and a skipped required check reads as passing: once the PR is marked ready, the bridge waits for the `ready_for_review` run's own success of `required-check` on that head.
2. **The ticket's contract gates the review.** The linked ticket must have a complete contract and every box outside `## Post deploy` ticked (`01-ticket-contract.md`). Otherwise the PR waits and its author is told once, on the PR and, for an incomplete contract, on the ticket too, once per set of missing parts, so that a held PR never waits unseen.
3. **The bridge opens the review.** When 1 and 2 hold, the bridge creates one **angle sub-item** per angle that applies to the PR plus one **consolidation item**, all linked to the PR: the PR's **review round**. The ticket is already In Review (1); the round's opening writes In Review only for a ticket sent back In Progress (§5), whose new head earns a new round. Being sub-items of the ticket, they are never taken for the PR's ticket, even when an agent mangled their marker (`01-ticket-contract.md` §7). The consolidation item is created with the round, so the whole round is visible from the start, but **blocked by every angle sub-item of the round** through the tracker's own `blocks` relation, which dispatchers respect: it is served only once every angle sub-item is resolved (Done or Canceled), when the tracker stops counting the relation as blocking (BR-ASDLC-018). The bridge assigns each item at its creation: an angle sub-item to its angle's `assignee`, the consolidation item to the coordinator named by `review.coordinator_assignee` (a participant the tracker knows, like an angle's reviewer; `08-configuration.md`); an unset or unknown participant leaves the item unassigned, and an existing item is never reassigned, since a human or an agent may have taken it. The relations are written right after the items and before the round comment (and In Review, for a ticket sent back); a relation that cannot be written holds the round's opening and is retried at the next tick, and an open round missing a relation gets it at the next tick. A round is scoped to its PR: a ticket already In Review for another of its PRs keeps its status and the new PR gets its own round. Which angles apply is configuration and the ticket's labels: the project's catalogue is `[[review.angles]]`, and an angle of it applies to a PR **whose ticket carries its label `angle/<key>`** (asked by hand, at refinement or later: a design pass, a security check), or **whose changed files touch its `paths`** (a security angle on the workflows and the authentication code; `paths = ["*"]` for every PR). Manual adds, never subtracts: a PR's angles are the ones its paths bring plus the ones its ticket's labels ask for, even when no file matches, and an angle with no `paths` applies only by its label. The label asks for the angle on every PR of the ticket; a path trigger is decided per PR, on that PR's diff. The size of the diff may add one more angle (possibly none at all, §11). Each configured angle has its label in the tracker: the bridge creates `angle/<key>` at its ticks when the tracker lacks it (where labels exist by being used, as Livela's tags, there is nothing to create), and `doctor` lists them. A ticket label `angle/<key>` whose key names no configured angle asks for nothing: the bridge says so once on the ticket (marker `spl-asdlc:unknown-angle-label label=<label>`) when it plans a PR of the ticket, and the PR is not held for it. A PR whose diff cannot be read gets every angle that carries paths, since whether it touches them is unknown, and is held until the diff is read. A PR that gets no angle has no round (§11).
    **Each item carries its protocol** (BR-ASDLC-015): the agent served it never depends on reading the method's repository, which is absent from the project's. Beside its contract (goal, non-goals, acceptance criteria, business rules), an item says what closes it in the bridge's terms and embeds, under a `## Protocol` section whose headings are demoted below the contract's, the method's protocol of its job: an angle sub-item `docs/agents/review-pr.md` and the angle's file `docs/agents/angles/<angle>.md` (an angle of the project's own, which the method has no file for, gets the generic protocol and a line saying no method notes exist for it); the consolidation item `docs/agents/consolidate.md`. The project's angle notes (`notes` of the angle) stay in the project's repository: the item links them. The texts are those of the version of spl-asdlc the project is pinned on, the ref of its workflow callers at the PR's base branch (`08-configuration.md`, "Angles", BR-ASDLC-032); texts that cannot be read there create no item, and the bridge says so on the ticket instead of copying other texts. An item keeps the texts it was created with: an item is found again by its marker, which holds no protocol text, or by its exact title among the ticket's children, so that an edited marker never makes the bridge create it twice, and is never rewritten. An angle sub-item closes when a submitted review on the PR names its key in its body (§4); the consolidation item closes with the round when the PR resolves (§6, §10).
4. **Findings live in the PR.** Each reviewer reads the diff from its angle, with the ticket's contract and the business rules touched as context, and posts its findings as review comments on the PR (one thread per finding, severity in the first line). A human who comments on the PR is one more angle. The tracker never receives a copy. An angle sub-item is done when the bridge closes it, once the reviewer's review is submitted, a review whose body names the sub-item's key, **or when it is resolved in the tracker** (Done or Canceled), by its reviewer or by anyone: a person reviewing from an angle assigned to them closes their sub-item there when their pass is done, and may push commits on the PR branch meanwhile (the coordinator approves the final head, §5). Either way it no longer holds the round (§6). A human review is that: an angle assigned to a person (`08-configuration.md` rule 12); there is no separate human-approval setting. The reviewer knows a PR may not close its ticket, and checks that it is deployable on its own (§10); a PR that is not self-standing, or that should have been one PR with another, is a `blocker` finding whose reason the reviewer also records in the ticket's comments.
5. **The coordinator fixes and approves.** Once every angle has submitted, the consolidation item is served: its blocking relations lift as the bridge closes each angle sub-item (§3, BR-ASDLC-018). The coordinator reads every thread, rejects false alarms with a reason in the thread, applies the retained fixes as commits on the PR branch, resolves the threads it has handled, and submits an approving review. If the change needed is beyond a fix (a confirmed finding that the PR is not deployable on its own, or should have been one PR, included), it requests changes and the ticket goes back to the implementer (In Progress), unless another PR of the ticket is in review (§10). The implementer pushes; the round of the new head, once its CI is green, brings the ticket back to In Review. On a PR with no angle (§11), a request for changes from a coordinator at the head sends the ticket back In Progress the same way, and the PR opened at a new head brings it back to In Review at once.
6. **CI again, then the bridge merges.** The merge requires, for a PR whose current plan gets an angle (§11): coordinator approval at the head SHA, no angle sub-item of the PR's round still open (an approval given while one is open does not count, even when a dispatcher ignored the relation; the PR is told once that it waits for the angle reviews, BR-ASDLC-018), required checks green at that SHA, no unresolved thread, branch up to date if required, no standing request for changes from a person, all green PRs before any failing or conflicting PR (one per base and tick when `review.require_up_to_date` holds). A request for changes stands, whatever the head it was given on, until it is dismissed or its author reviews again (GitHub keeps it blocking the merge). Besides the coordinators' own (which hold the PR, §5): a **person's** holds the merge, and the bridge never dismisses it; the tick says `waiting: pr#<n> changes requested by <login> (a person; the bridge never dismisses it)`. An **App's** (an author `<slug>[bot]` once normalised, as the coordinators are matched) is superseded by the coordinator's approval at the head: when every other condition holds, the bridge dismisses each such review right before the merge, with a message naming the coordinator's approval and the head SHA, then merges. Without that approval nothing is dismissed. Each dismissal is in the action log and the dry run; a dismissed review is no longer standing, so a replay dismisses nothing again, and a dismissal that fails holds the merge until the next tick. The merge is a squash by default (configurable), pinned to the head SHA; the branch is deleted; the PR's round closes; the ticket goes where §10 says: **To Deploy** when every box is ticked and no other PR is open (**Done** for a ticket with nothing to deploy). The merge comment on the ticket states only the conditions that applied: green; approved by the coordinator when the PR had a review round (a PR with no angle, §11, says it was merged with no PR review); up to date with its base when `review.require_up_to_date` holds.
7. **The merge is idempotent and observable.** The bridge records every merge decision in its action log; a PR merged outside the bridge is swept and given the same transitions, once.
8. **Exclusions.** PRs of the release machinery (merge-backs and their conflict resolutions, angle PRs of a release QA) are never reviewed by angles and never merged by this path; `05-release-cycle.md` governs them.
9. **PRs of Apps that need no ticket.** A PR is linked to exactly one ticket, which a human or an agent writes; a PR authored by an App the project lists (`review.ticketless_authors`, for example Dependabot) gets its ticket from the bridge instead. When such a PR is linked to no ticket, the bridge opens one: labelled `ticketless-pr`, with a complete contract whose one box the PR meets, linked to the PR. From the next tick the PR takes the path of every PR (review round, merge, To Deploy, release, closure). Only an App qualifies, never a human whose login matches. When such a PR is closed without merge (an App superseding its own PR), the bridge cancels the ticket it opened and that ticket's open sub-items; a PR reopened later stays on that ticket until a person sets it back to Ready. Any other PR without a ticket waits, and its author is told once.
10. **A ticket and its PRs.** One PR per ticket is the default, with as many commits as needed; several only for a real reason, recorded by the implementer in the ticket's comments. **Every PR branches from its base** (`main`, or the release branch for a hotfix) and is never stacked on another PR's branch; a ticket's later PR also starts from the base, once the earlier one is merged. With a squash merge, a stacked PR carries the other PR's diff into the base under its own merge, and the bridge never sees that other PR, nor its ticket, merged. **Every PR is independently deployable**: a merged PR ships with the next release whatever its ticket's state, even when it does not close the ticket; the release embarks the ticket and the closure judges it only once it is To Deploy (`05-release-cycle.md` §11). **A review round is scoped to its PR**: its angle sub-items and its consolidation item close when the PR resolves (Done at a merge, Canceled at a close without merge), while the ticket stays open for a later PR. Whenever a PR of a ticket resolves, and whenever a ticket past the merge (To Deploy, Done) has an open PR, the bridge sets the ticket's state by these rules, in order:
    1. **The review rule** has priority: another PR of the ticket in review (opened, not sent back; its round open or not yet opened) keeps or puts the ticket **In Review**, and that PR's review → deploy cycle runs in full; a PR of the ticket the coordinator sent back (changes requested at its head) puts it **In Progress**.
    2. **The completeness rule**: with no PR in review, a ticket whose boxes are not all ticked (every box of its description under any heading, the `## Post deploy` section set apart; a ticket that declares no box is not complete) goes back **In Progress**. An implementer resumes; its next PR brings the ticket to In Review again, until every box is ticked.
    3. Otherwise, a PR of the ticket merged: **To Deploy**, waiting for the next release (every project has the cycle, `05-release-cycle.md`); none merged (a PR closed without merge): **In Progress**.

    **Nothing to deploy.** A ticket that carries the no-deploy label (`tracker.no_deploy_label`, default `no-deploy`; a person or refinement adds it, the bridge only reads it) has nothing to deploy, documentation say: its merge delivers it. Where rule 3 says To Deploy it goes **Done**, by the Done gate the closure uses (its boxes and its duplicates'), or **Post Deploy**, where the ticket itself is validated in production (BR-ASDLC-003), while only its own `## Post deploy` boxes are open, or In Progress while a box of a duplicate is open; and no release embarks it (`05-release-cycle.md` §2): no release label, no judgement at the closure. The release whose range holds its merge announces it in its note and its closure archives it with the cycle (BR-ASDLC-019). Without the label, To Deploy as above.

    **A box checked after the merge.** A ticket left **In Progress** past a merge (a PR merged while a box was open, or the closure sent it back) moves on at the next tick once that box is checked: with no PR of it open (a draft does not count), every box outside `## Post deploy` ticked and a merge recorded by the bridge, the rules above put it **To Deploy** while its last merged PR waits for a release, or Done / Post Deploy as above with nothing to deploy. When a release tag already holds that PR (the closure sent it back, or a release went out while it was In Progress), the bridge judges it as the closure does (`05-release-cycle.md` §11): **Done**, or **Post Deploy** while its own `## Post deploy` boxes wait; never To Deploy, which no later closure would judge. A ticket citing rules the registry at that tag lacks stays In Progress, said once. The transition is commented once (`state:`, `next:`, marker `spl-asdlc:settled pr=<url> state=<state>`), then written; a ticket with a box still open stays where it is and nothing is said.

    A draft PR is none of the ticket's open PRs here: it is out of the flow (§1). A ticket in Post Deploy is not reopened. When the ticket of an open PR could not be read, it may be this ticket's: nothing but "another PR is in review" can be said, so the bridge writes nothing and decides at the next tick.
11. **Review is optional per project.** A project may run the method with no PR review: it writes `angles = []` under `[review]`, an explicit choice (a file that says nothing about its angles is invalid, `08-configuration.md`). A PR whose plan gets **no angle** — none whose label its ticket carries, none whose paths it touches, none added by its size — has **no review round**: the bridge creates no angle sub-item and no consolidation item, and needs no coordinator approval; its ticket went In Review when the PR was opened, as any (§1). It merges as soon as it is mergeable on its CI: required checks green (1), the ticket's contract complete with a box ticked (2), the business-rule guard, nothing that holds it (an unread diff or a skipped required check), no unresolved thread, the branch up to date when required, all green PRs before any failing or conflicting PR (one per base and tick when `review.require_up_to_date` holds); a standing request for changes from a configured coordinator, or from a person, still holds it; with no approval, nothing is dismissed (§6). The merge, the deleted branch and the ticket's state follow §6 and §10 as for any PR (To Deploy when every box is ticked); the action log, and so `tick` and its dry run, say the merge was made with no PR review. Angles that labels and triggers add still apply: a PR whose ticket carries an angle's label, that touches the `paths` of an angle, or that is over a configured size threshold (off by default), gets its round and needs the coordinator's approval, so `review.coordinators` is required whenever some PR can get an angle (whenever the project has a review angle or a size threshold). `doctor` reports `PR review: none` when no PR can. **The plan is the current configuration's and labels'**: it is computed at every tick from the configuration the bridge reads then and the ticket's labels then, and the merge conditions follow it, never the configuration or the labels a round was opened under. So **removing an angle's label cancels that angle** when only the label asked for it (a deliberate human gesture, like putting a PR back in draft), while an angle the PR's paths bring stays whatever the labels say: an angle that fires too often is narrowed in the configuration's `paths`, never removed from a ticket. A PR whose plan gets no angle merges on its CI as above **whatever round is open**: a round opened under an earlier configuration (before `angles = []`, say) no longer applies, and the bridge cancels its open items — angle sub-items and consolidation item — before the merge, each with a comment saying neither the configuration nor the ticket's labels ask for it any more; the coordinator's approval is then not required. A PR whose plan gets fewer angles than its open round has keeps the rest of its round: the angle sub-items of the angles no longer asked are canceled the same way, the others and the consolidation item stand, and the coordinator's approval is still required (§6). An angle is taken as no longer asked only on a diff read in full: an unread diff matched no angle's paths, so it cancels nothing. Each item is commented once and then Canceled; a cancel that fails holds the merge until the next tick, which never comments the item again. Release QA is configured on its own (`05-release-cycle.md` §3): no PR review says nothing about a release's angles.

## What the reviewer receives

- The diff, the PR description, the linked ticket's goal and acceptance criteria, and the ticket's comments, where its implementer records why it has more than one PR (§10).
- The business rules touched by the diff (the rule files of `.spl-asdlc/business-rules/` the diff changes, or whose test files or cited paths it intersects) and the registry id format.
- Its angle protocol (`docs/agents/review-pr.md` and `docs/agents/angles/<angle>.md`), embedded in its sub-item (§3), and a link to the project's angle notes if any.
- The severity scale and the verdict vocabulary (`03-vocabulary.md`).

## What the coordinator receives

Everything the reviewers received, plus every review thread, the CI status, and the consolidation protocol (`docs/agents/consolidate.md`, embedded in the consolidation item, §3): every finding is confirmed or rejected with a reason, exactly once; a rejected finding is answered in its thread; a confirmed one is fixed on the branch or sent back to the implementer.

## Configuration (per project)

- The angles (`angles = []` for none: no PR review, §11), each asked by its ticket label `angle/<key>` or brought by its `paths` (§3), each with an optional assignee hint: an agent the tracker serves, or a person for a human review.
- The coordinators whose approval merges a reviewed PR (`review.coordinators`), required only when some PR can get an angle (§11), and the tracker participant who receives the consolidation item (`review.coordinator_assignee`), optional.
- Merge method (`squash` | `merge`), whether the branch must be up to date, the diff size above which an extra angle is added (none by default).
- The Apps whose PRs need no ticket (§9).
